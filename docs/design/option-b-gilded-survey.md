# Gilded Survey

A design philosophy for the second treatment of inc.aglc.sa ("Option B").

## The movement

Gilded Survey treats the act of entering a market the way a nineteenth-century cartographer treated a coastline: as something unknown that becomes navigable only through patient, exact measurement. The page is a chart. Deep navy is the sea of the unmapped; thin gold lines are the soundings, bearings and fixed points a surveyor laid down one by one. Nothing on the surface is decorative. Every rule, dot and numeral marks a position in a journey from inquiry to registered company, and the viewer should sense that the chart was drawn by someone who has crossed the water many times.

## Space and form

Space is held open with the confidence of a document that has nothing to hide. Large quiet fields of navy and warm paper alternate like land and water, and content sits on them in narrow, deliberate bands. Forms are rectilinear and hairline: survey grids, concentric arcs of range, tick marks along an edge, a single filled circle where a phase is complete. Photography, when it appears, is a horizon seen at dusk, darkened until the city reads as a silhouette of towers against a gold sky. Rounded corners are rare and small; the geometry is ruled, not drawn freehand. The effect must look meticulously crafted, every alignment the product of countless refinements by a master hand.

## Color and material

Three materials only. Ink navy (#0b1120 to #182440) is the ground. Gold (#b8923a, lifted to #e3c97a on dark ground) is the instrument: it draws lines, numbers and the one action the viewer is asked to take. Paper (#fbfaf7 to #e9e4d8) is the daylight page where fees and facts are laid out. Gold is never a fill for large areas; it is a hairline, a numeral, a 2px underline, a thin frame around a portrait. White text on navy is set at 75% opacity for body copy and full strength only for headings, so the page breathes like a dark room lit by a single lamp.

## Scale and rhythm

Headlines are large and serifed, set tight, one thought per line, and they are the only loud element on any screen. Everything else is small: 11px uppercase tracked labels that behave like map annotations ("01 · ELIGIBILITY", "REF. INQ-2026-J-DRVG"), 15px body copy, hairline rules. Rhythm comes from repetition at exact intervals: the five phases as five evenly spaced nodes on a line; the 32 services as a ledger with a ruled baseline every 44px; stats as four equal columns separated by 1px gold verticals. The composition rewards slow reading, as a scientific plate rewards a magnifying glass; this density of fine marks is what signals painstaking attention.

## Composition and balance

Each section is composed around one strong horizontal — a line of towers, a ruled baseline, a gold rule under a title — with content balanced asymmetrically against it: heavy text on one side, a measured instrument (a card, a portrait, a diagram) on the other. Motion is a surveyor's reveal: elements fade and rise 12px as they enter the viewport, nothing bounces, nothing loops. Reduced-motion viewers see the finished chart at once. The result should feel like a document that took many hours to draw and would hang comfortably in the firm's own office: restrained, exact, and unmistakably the product of deep expertise.

## Hierarchy

Three levels and no more. The headline and its gold accent. The labelled instrument (figure, card, diagram, portrait). The quiet supporting line. If an element cannot be placed in one of these three, it does not belong on the chart.
